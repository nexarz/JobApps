Please describe a time where you developed and delivered a project. What was your role? How did you communicate with team members or colleagues? What was the outcome? 



I am a strong advocate for equity, shaped by lived experiences and my work with racialized communities. As a Community Schools Coordinator at the Vancouver School Board during the COVID-19 pandemic, I witnessed firsthand how systemic barriers disproportionately impacted the children and families I supported—ranging from food insecurity to a lack of remote learning support, to direct experiences of racism and hate.

Beyond addressing immediate needs, I saw an opportunity for long-term systemic change. I founded my department’s first Anti-Racism and Inclusion Committee and led the design and delivery of its first anti-racism training for staff and volunteers. As Committee Lead, I collaborated with school staff, community partners, and families to conduct needs assessments, ensuring our initiatives were responsive and inclusive. To reduce participation barriers, I adapted my communication strategies, providing translation support and childcare while balancing formal and informal engagement approaches.  

Cultural safety was central to my leadership. Recognizing the emotional toll of anti-racism work, I encouraged my racialized team members to prioritize well-being. The committee’s key outcomes included anti-racism training delivered to over 250 staff and volunteers and the development of an equity audit framework used in over 50 schools to assess programs and services. These efforts advanced the school board’s commitment to equity, fostering safer, more inclusive environments for racialized students and their communities.  

Please describe a time you prepared a policy brief or report with recommendations. What was your process for writing the brief? How did you arrive at your recommendations?



When I started as the Inclusion Planner with the City of Richmond, advancing anti-racism was part of my portfolio but was embedded within broader diversity, equity, and inclusion work. Recognizing the need to distinguish anti-racism as a strategic priority, I prepared a policy brief with recommendations for my manager and department head.

I began by building a strong local case using census data and reports to analyze Richmond’s demographics and race-based data, highlighting the lived experiences of racialized residents and the need for an explicit anti-racism focus. I then conducted a jurisdictional scan of municipalities across Canada, engaging with fellow planners to identify best practices. To ensure alignment with broader policy frameworks, I examined provincial and federal initiatives such as British Columbia’s Anti-Racism Act and Canada’s Anti-Racism Strategy. Research from the CRRF also informed my understanding of national trends.

I grounded my recommendations in data, aligning them with the City’s strategic directions while emphasizing the opportunity to lead in anti-racism work and remain accountable to provincial and federal efforts. My brief proposed short- and long-term actions, including an anti-racism webpage for resources, a community-driven response to racism and hate, and policy integration across departments. As a result, anti-racism has been increasingly recognized as a priority, shaping and strengthening the City’s commitment to racial equity.







Please describe your approach for managing and maintaining stakeholder relations. What is your communication style? Do you have an example of a time you managed a challenging or sensitive relationship? Please provide details.

Building and maintaining stakeholder relationships is essential for advancing public policy. Having allies and partners in the policy planning process has helped shape and inform my work, leading to more well-rounded research findings. My approach for managing and maintaining stakeholder relations is centred on proactive engagement, transparency, and responsiveness. I establish trust by keeping stakeholders informed through regular updates, sharing relevant resources, and ensuring open channels for dialogue. This ongoing communication fosters strong partnerships, making seeking input easier, collaborating on shared goals, and navigating challenges effectively. My communication style is collaborative, calm, and assertive. When conversing, I confidently express my views while actively listening to others. In today’s polarized climate, my ability to remain attentive and open-minded helps me build consensus and maintain positive relationships, even in sensitive discussions.

At the City of Richmond, I serve as the Staff Liaison to the Council-appointed Intercultural Advisory Committee, managing relationships with community members who are advocating for intercultural harmony. Balancing the City’s interests with diverse community perspectives can be sensitive, especially when concerns arise about municipal responsibilities. While some issues align with the City’s role, others require collaboration across government levels and community groups. I actively listen, acknowledge valid concerns, and clarify the City’s jurisdiction with transparency and empathy. This approach has helped me build trust with committee members and guide discussions toward collaborative, actionable solutions. By fostering mutual understanding and focusing on shared goals, I ensure that no concern is dismissed while keeping discussions grounded in practical steps forward.

Please identify two emerging public policy priorities impacting race relations in Canada, and write a small brief about the priorities, concluding the brief with 1-2 policy recommendations the CRRF should consider implementing. 

Two emerging public policy priorities impacting race relations in Canada are online hate and the need for enhanced anti-racism supports for newcomers in light of Canada’s 2025-2027 Immigration Levels Plan.

Online HateThe rise of online hate speech poses a significant threat to racial harmony, digital safety, media literacy, and social cohesion in Canada. As outlined in Canada’s Anti-Racism Strategy, white supremacist and far-right groups increasingly use digital platforms to spread disinformation and sow divisions among equity-deserving communities. This not only fosters exclusion and discrimination but also leads to real-world consequences, including hate crimes. Social media platforms such as TikTok and Facebook serve as echo chambers, amplifying extremist views and enabling hate groups to organize. These online dynamics erode Canada’s values of multiculturalism and inclusion.

Policy Recommendation: While Canada has made strides through its Action Plan on Combatting Hate, CRRF should advocate for further strengthening online hate legislation. This could include refining the definition of online hate speech through a racial equity lens, collaborating with public policy advisors at platforms like Meta to improve awareness of reporting mechanisms, and expanding digital literacy programs to help Canadians identify and counter online disinformation and hate speech.

Anti-Racism Supports for NewcomersCommunities across Canada are starting to experience the impacts of the 2025-2027 Immigration Levels Plan. With decreased funding for many settlement service agencies, both staff and newcomers face a growing gap in essential services, which directly affects newcomers and their well-being. Immigrants and refugees continue to arrive in Canada, necessitating sustained settlement support, including enhanced anti-racism initiatives. Newcomers frequently encounter systemic barriers and discrimination, which can hinder their integration. However, with limited resources and capacity, settlement agencies cannot adequately support their clients to address the intersections of race, culture, and immigration status.

Policy Recommendation: With the current Immigration Levels Plan already in effect and many funding decisions by IRCC finalized, CRRF should consider advocating for increased anti-racism support for newcomers through alternative means. One approach is to add newcomers as a priority focus within the CRRF’s National Anti-Racism Fund or federal initiatives such as the Multiculturalism and Anti-Racism Program. This would help build capacity among general social service providers, ensuring racialized newcomers have access to culturally appropriate supports beyond traditional settlement agencies and receive the necessary resources to navigate systemic barriers and respond to racism and hate.
